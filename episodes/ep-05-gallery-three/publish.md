## Title

The Operator Went Into Gallery Three Alone | FC9-LOG-005

## Description

This record is fiction. Daedalus Two, its crew and its logs are invented; the length of the lunar night, the far side's lack of line of sight to Earth, and the energy arithmetic below are real and can be checked.

Recovered suit recorder, Daedalus Two, lunar far side. Six entries. One operator on shift for a night that lasts about 354 hours.

Chapters
0:00 Night hour zero
0:30 The budget
1:05 Gallery three
1:45 Telemetry
2:25 Bus three
3:05 Night hour thirty-one
3:30 Record ends

What is real
- A lunar day equals the synodic month, 29.530589 days. Near the equator night is about half of it: 14.765 days, or 354.37 hours.
- The far side never faces Earth (apart from small libration at the limb), so a station there has no direct radio line to Earth. At full moon on Earth, the far side is in night.
- Daedalus is a real far-side crater near the lunar equator.
- Energy is power × time. 4000 kWh at 11.0 kW lasts 363.64 h, a margin of 9.27 h over the night. Thirty hours at 11.0 kW should leave 3670 kWh; a reading of 3658 kWh means an extra 0.40 kW. At 11.4 kW the battery empties at night hour 350.9. Isolated back to 11.0 kW at hour 31, it would have lasted to hour 362.5.

What is invented
The station, crew, battery size, survival draw, the void at 41 m, protocol seven, the suit telemetry, the relay loss, bus three, and one reading that should not exist.

The record ends where the recording ends.
