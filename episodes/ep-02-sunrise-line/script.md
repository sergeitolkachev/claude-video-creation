## Synopsis

A world that turns once every 366 Earth days. Its habitable zone is a
sixty-kilometre band of ground that walks around the equator at 2.5 km/h,
and everything alive on Aldren walks with it, forever, or burns. The record
is a survey of that arithmetic. The animal that lives inside it is shown
once, for three seconds, and never explained.

## Prompt Hygiene (applies when building `shots.yaml`)

Carried forward from record 1, plus what this world adds.

- No grain, no scan lines, no dropout, no "found footage" in any prompt.
  Degradation is an ffmpeg pass at the end. Prompting it wastes generation.
- No readable text, no numbers, no labels, no signage in frame. Every
  character on screen is drawn by Pillow in post, in the channel's Menlo.
- The animal appears in exactly one generated frame, 7.2. Nowhere else does
  a prompt mention an animal, a body, a leg or an eye. Scene 4's herd is
  "distant dark shapes in haze, unresolved" and must stay unresolved.
- No sky detail. Thin haze, no clouds, no stars, no second sun. The sun sits
  **on** the horizon and never above it — a model given "sunrise" will lift
  it, so every prompt says low and level, never rising.
- **The animal's passage is a band, never a mark.** Five generation routes
  were spent trying to produce a single impression in soft ground; every one
  returned a boot sole, a tyre rut, a tracked belt or a moulded bowl, and
  naming those to forbid them drew them. The words "track", "print" and
  "footprint" are out of every prompt in this episode. What is described
  instead is a continuous band of broken, pressed-down crust with untouched
  ground beside it — which is what an animal that never stops walking
  actually leaves.
- Seedream rewrites every prompt before generating and cannot be turned off.
  Anything vague comes back as whatever is statistically typical: "dust"
  became heaps of grit on record 1, and "plain" invites a desert with dunes.
  Say flat, say fine-grained, say the dust is airborne and lateral.

## Appendix — number provenance

Separated deliberately. Everything in the first list is real and checkable;
everything in the second is invented for Aldren but derived from the first.
All of it was re-derived and checked before the shot list was written.

**Real anchors**
- A slowly rotating body genuinely produces a walking-pace terminator.
  Mercury's solar day is about 176 Earth days and its equatorial
  circumference roughly 15,300 km, giving a terminator ground speed near
  3.6 km/h. Venus's solar day is about 117 Earth days. Aldren is not more
  exotic than the two nearest examples.
- Terminator speed scaling with cos(latitude) is plain geometry, not a
  modelling assumption.
- Basal metabolic rate from Kleiber's law: BMR ≈ 70·M^0.75 kcal/day. At
  200 kg this gives ≈ 3,723 kcal/day ≈ 15.6 MJ/day. Rounded to 16.
- Cost of transport from Taylor's allometry: minimum COT ≈ 10.7·M^−0.316
  J/(kg·m). At 200 kg this gives ≈ 2.01 J/(kg·m), so ≈ 401 J per metre, and
  60 km/day works out to ≈ 24.1 MJ/day. The claim that locomotion outweighs
  basal cost falls out of the arithmetic — it was not chosen for effect.
  This allometry is measured on legged runners, and the animal here is not
  one; a body that moves on its underside pays more per metre, not less, so
  24 MJ/day is used as a conservative floor rather than an estimate. The
  narration says "about" and states no total beyond 40.
- Unihemispheric slow-wave sleep is documented in cetaceans and in birds
  sleeping on the wing.
- Earth–Moon mean distance 384,400 km.

**Invented for this world**
- Aldren's radius (3,500 km), solar day (366 Earth days), axial tilt (1.4°).
- The 60 km width of the survivable band, and therefore the 24-hour margin.
- Adult mass 200 kg and 40-year lifespan.
- The animal itself, its nursery latitude, and its poleward drift with age.
- Its body plan: a legless body four metres long lying flat against the
  ground, moving on its underside, leaving a continuous band the width of
  itself. Nothing on Earth at 200 kg moves this way. It was chosen because a
  legged animal leaves separate prints and this record is built on an
  unbroken trail — the storyboard and the biology disagreed, and the biology
  was the part that had not been decided yet.

Derived figures, if any input above is changed: circumference = 2πR;
terminator speed = circumference ÷ solar day; margin = band width ÷
terminator speed; lifetime distance = terminator speed × 24 × 365.25 ×
lifespan.

**Checked 2026-09-09**, every figure recomputed from the inputs above:
circumference 21,991 km; terminator 2.504 km/h; margin 23.97 h; lifetime
877,843 km (narration says "roughly 878,000"); cosine rows 2.50 / 2.17 /
1.77 / 1.25 / 0.65 / 0.00; Kleiber 15.58 MJ; Taylor 24.07 MJ; Mercury
3.63 km/h. Nothing in the narration drifts from this list.
