REAL, AND CHECKABLE

Lake Vostok: about 250 km long, 50 km at its widest, 12,500 km2, average
depth 432 m. Surface roughly 4,000 m below the ice surface and 500 m below
sea level. Hydrostatic pressure at the lake surface is reported at about
345-355 bar. Water temperature is calculated at about -3 C.

Why it is liquid: the melting point of ice falls about 0.0074 K per bar, so
350 bar depresses it by 2.6 K, to -2.6 C. The lake is liquid because of the
weight on it.

Isolation is estimated at about 15 million years, with a range of 15-25.
The effective mean residence time of the water, though, is 13,300 years -
the lake is old, its water is not. The coda uses that on purpose.

Gas: the overlying ice carries air in clathrates, and melting into the lake
releases it. The water is estimated at about 2.5 litres of nitrogen and
oxygen per kilogram, roughly 50x an ordinary surface freshwater lake. Modelled
by McKay et al., Geophysical Research Letters, 2003.

The 730 mg/L in this record is derived, not quoted: freshwater at 0 C
saturates at about 14.6 mg O2/L, and 50 x 14.6 = 730 mg/L, or 22.8 mmol/L.

Organic carbon: dissolved organic carbon measured in Vostok accretion ice is
on the order of 79-510 ug/L, with 86 ug/L the commonly cited value - about
7 umol C/L. If you go looking for this, note that at least one secondary
rendering of the Siegert 2001 review prints these as mg/L. That is a unit
error. 79-510 mg/L of DOC would be hundreds of times richer than seawater.

Electron bookkeeping: reducing one O2 to two H2O takes 4 electrons.
Oxidising one carbon atom releases 4. One carbon per one O2, exactly. That is
stoichiometry, not an estimate, and it is what makes the ledger in this
record checkable rather than rhetorical.

No clean sample exists. The Russian team reached the lake in 2012; in 2017
they reported the recovered water was too contaminated with drilling fluid
for reliable chemistry or biology. That is the last card in the file and it
is true.

INVENTED

The animal: a 120 mm sphere with a 40 mm living core - 96% shield by volume,
871 g of rind - carrying 10 mol of oxidisable carbon. That it does not feed.
That no thin-bodied form has been recorded.

THE ARITHMETIC

Influx through a spherical shell: J = 4 pi D dC R r / (R - r). With
D = 1.0e-9 m2/s, dC = 22.8 mol/m3, R = 0.060 m, r = 0.020 m, that is
8.6e-9 mol/s - 0.742 mmol/day, 0.271 mol/year.

Break-even needs carbon influx to equal oxygen influx in moles. Through a
non-selective barrier influx scales as D x C, and carbon diffuses about three
times slower, so the water would need 3.33 x 22.8 = 76 mmol/L of dissolved
carbon. Measured: 0.007 mmol/L. A deficit of 10,600x.

Even the most generous published inference - up to 1,200 ug/L for the lake
water itself, or 100 umol/L - still leaves a deficit of 760x. The conclusion
does not depend on which figure you use, which is why the record uses the
measured one.

Shield life: the rind is eaten from the inside, so the path shortens and the
arrival rate climbs. Dividing the store by the starting rate gives 37 years
and is wrong by a factor of five. Integrating properly gives 7.1 years. Both
numbers are on screen, because 37 is what you get if you check it the quick
way.
