REAL, AND CHECKABLE

Mass in flight: m = F / a. Guidance systems estimate vehicle mass during a burn from the thrust and the measured acceleration, and use it to cross-check the propellant budget.

The engine: 24.0 kN at a specific impulse of 320 s, so an exhaust velocity of 3,138 m/s and a propellant flow of 7.65 kg/s. A 40 s correction uses 306 kg.

Why a bias cancels: thrust is set by chamber pressure through the nozzle, F = C_F x p_c x A_t. With the same engine and the same chamber pressure to 0.05 percent, two burns have the same thrust to 0.05 percent, whatever its absolute error. A 1.5 percent uncertainty in thrust is 374-379 kg at this mass; the change between two burns is known to about 13 kg.

A sealed ship's mass does not change except through what leaves it. Food, water and waste moved around inside change where the mass is, not how much there is.

Gimbal trim: the engine is steered so its thrust passes through the center of mass. A trim angle t off prediction, with the gimbal 9.0 m from the center of mass, is an offset of 9.0 x tan t. A mass X added at distance r moves the center of mass by X x r / M.

The rocket equation: delta-v = v_e x ln(m0 / m1).
