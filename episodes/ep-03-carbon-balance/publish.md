# Publication — FC9-003 // CARBON BALANCE

Everything that gets pasted into YouTube lives here, versioned like the script
and for the same reason: a description re-typed from memory in a year is a
different description, and the "what is real" section is the only place the
channel puts its credibility in writing.

Records 01 and 02 have no file like this. Their descriptions exist only in
Studio.

Rules that apply to every record on this channel:

- **The disclaimer is the first line.** Not the second, not after a hook. It is
  what makes the rest of the description readable as fiction rather than as a
  claim.
- **The real figures are listed, and they are actually real.** The genre lives
  on numbers that check out; a viewer with a calculator is the audience, not a
  hazard. Everything in the "verifiable" list below can be checked.
- **The twist is never explained**, in the description any more than in the
  narration.

## LONG RECORD

### Title

```
FC9-003 // CARBON BALANCE
```

### Description

```
Work of fiction. All records, objects and events are invented. Any resemblance
to real missions is coincidental. The physics is real and is listed below.

Relay station PERIGON-4. One operator. The carbon dioxide scrubber is eating
cartridges faster than specification, and he does what the form tells him to
do: logs a defect, then a second defect, then stops calling it a defect and
measures it.

Recovered log entries 112 to 121.


THE ARITHMETIC, IF YOU WANT TO CHECK IT

Ideal gas: dP = mRT / MV. One kilogram of CO2 in 84 m3 at 295 K is 663.6 Pa,
which is 4.977 mmHg, which spread over a day is 0.2074 mmHg per hour. The
measured 0.29 mmHg/h divides out to 1.398 kg/day.

A human produces on the order of 1.0 kg of CO2 per crew-day — the standard
figure used to size life support. About 1 kg/day corresponds to roughly 100 W
of resting metabolism, so 0.43 kg/day is about 43 W.

Lithium hydroxide: 2 LiOH + CO2 -> Li2CO3 + H2O. 47.9 g of LiOH per 44.0 g of
CO2, so roughly 0.92 kg of CO2 per kg of LiOH in theory; real hardware runs
lower.

CO2 partial pressure: sea-level air is about 0.3 mmHg, ISS operational range is
roughly 2-4 mmHg, and 7.6 mmHg is one per cent of a 760 mmHg atmosphere.

Exponential fit: 0.28 to 0.43 over eleven days gives k = 0.0390 per day, a
doubling time of 17.77 days, and 21.6 days from 0.43 to 1.00.

INVENTED: the station, the operator, every log number, the 84 m3 pressurised
volume and its four sections, the 2.6 kg cartridge capacity, the scrubber rated
at 2.0 kg/day, the operator's 81 kg, and the nine-day silence from control.


0:00  LOG 112 — nothing to report
0:22  LOG 114 — two defects in a row
0:50  LOG 117 — the scrubber, switched off
1:30  LOG 118 — four sections, four bulkheads
1:55  LOG 119 — recalculated from the cartridge record
2:27  LOG 120 — the manifest
2:57  LOG 121 — nothing to report
```

### Notes for whoever pastes it

- The chapter list must stay last: YouTube reads timestamps from anywhere in
  the description, and a stray `0:00` earlier in the text silently becomes
  chapter one.
- Timestamps are scene boundaries summed from `shots.yaml`. If a scene is
  retimed, they move — do not copy them from an older record.
- No links in the first two lines. The disclaimer has to be what is visible
  before "show more".

## VERTICAL CUTS

each carries its own one-line disclaimer. They do not carry the arithmetic —
there is no room and no reason, and the long record is where it lives.

```
Title:  He switched off the scrubber to find out. FC9-003

Fiction. He runs the CO2 scrubber offline for six hours and measures the rise
himself. At 84 m3 and 295 K, one kilogram a day should raise partial pressure
by 0.21 mmHg per hour. He measured 0.29.
```

### Notes

- **Titles are the operator's arithmetic, never a question.** "What is in
  section D?" is a different channel. The title states what he did; the
  strangeness is in what he found, and the viewer has to stay for it.
- `.d`'s description is the closest any of these come to explaining the record,
  and it stops one sentence short on purpose — it says the output is growing
  and does not say what grows.
- Every vertical ends on the channel card, so the description does not have to
  do the asking twice.
